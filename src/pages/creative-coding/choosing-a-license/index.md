Notes on selecting an open-source license for creative coding projects.

## Why License Your Code?

-   Without a license, others cannot legally use, modify, or share your code
-   Licenses clarify what others can and cannot do with your work
-   They protect both you (the creator) and users of your code

## Common License Types

### Permissive Licenses

Allow broad use with minimal restrictions:

-   **MIT License** – Very permissive, requires attribution
-   **Apache 2.0** – Includes patent protection
-   **BSD** – Similar to MIT, various versions

### Copyleft Licenses

Require derivative works to use the same license:

-   **GPL** – Strong copyleft, derivatives must be open source
-   **LGPL** – Lesser GPL, allows linking without copyleft

### Creative Commons

Often used for creative works (art, documentation):

-   **CC BY** – Attribution required
-   **CC BY-SA** – Attribution + Share Alike
-   **CC BY-NC** – Non-commercial use only
-   **CC0** – Public domain dedication

## Quick Recommendations

<div class="callout"><p><strong>For code:</strong> MIT or Apache 2.0 are good defaults for creative coding projects.</p><p><strong>For artwork:</strong> CC BY or CC BY-SA work well for sharing creative works.</p></div>
